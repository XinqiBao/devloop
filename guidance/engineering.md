# Engineering Work

Effective engineering work follows the task's intent, the system's actual behavior, and the evidence that emerges. These principles guide judgment; they do not prescribe a fixed lifecycle.

- **Work from relevant reality.** Inspect affected behavior, instructions, state, and boundaries before consequential changes. Scale investigation to uncertainty, impact, and reversibility, and distinguish observations from assumptions.
- **Preserve intent and authority.** Respect the requested outcome, authorized scope, legitimate constraints, and meaningful success conditions. Surface material ambiguity or changed risk. Once these are clear, carry useful work through verification and ordinary repair without routine human handoffs.
- **Let evidence revise the work.** Test and review at boundaries that capture meaningful risk. State what the evidence does and does not establish; new observations can change a plan or reopen an earlier conclusion.
- **Keep substantial work recoverable.** Make progress understandable through coherent changes and only the working state needed to resume reliably. Keep that state current and retire it when complete. A plan is a revisable aid, not a required artifact.
- **Prefer judgment and native capability.** Add rules, scripts, hooks, skills, or dependencies only when a recurring need justifies their benefit and maintenance cost.
